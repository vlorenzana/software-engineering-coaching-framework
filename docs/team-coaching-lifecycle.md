# SECAV-O Team Coaching Lifecycle

## Principle

SECAV-O treats coaching as part of team formation and evolution, not as a remedial activity that begins only after performance problems appear. Coaching should begin at the earliest practical team inception event—planning, kickoff, launch, formation, or an equivalent ceremony—and continue as the team changes.

## 1. Team inception / planning / launch

The coach participates early enough to understand and help communicate:

- institutional and project objectives;
- project scope and expected outcomes;
- key stakeholders and roles;
- architecture / technical context where relevant;
- engineering workflow and delivery cadence;
- quality, review, inspection and unit-test expectations;
- AI-assistance and human-validation expectations;
- known project and coaching risks;
- evidence and metrics that may be collected; and
- initial competency-development priorities.

The coach does not replace the project manager, engineering manager, architect, product owner or risk owner. The coach uses this context to align coaching objectives and observations with the work the team is expected to perform.

## 2. Member preparation before launch

Before or during launch, team members should receive a preparation activity appropriate to their roles. Preparation may include briefings, practice activities, reference material, review of standards, guided examples, or targeted training. The goal is to reduce avoidable ambiguity and identify support needs before delivery begins.

Preparation should cover, where applicable:

- project and institutional objectives;
- critical risks and preventive actions;
- development and quality practices;
- peer review / inspection responsibilities;
- unit-test expectations;
- secure-development expectations;
- AI-assisted development rules and human-validation responsibilities;
- relevant tools, artifacts, acceptance criteria and evidence; and
- how coaching evidence will be used and protected.

## 3. Initial growth baseline

An initial baseline is established for each member where practical. It may draw from direct observation, prior work products, structured assessment, review/inspection performance, unit-test evidence, defect data, self-assessment and manager/coach context. The baseline should identify both demonstrated strengths and development needs.

Baseline dimensions may include:

- competency level for relevant engineering activities;
- degree of autonomy;
- ability to review others' work;
- recurring review/inspection findings;
- unit-test design and execution evidence;
- defect injection/detection patterns where attributable and appropriate;
- familiarity with project architecture, domain and quality requirements; and
- responsible use and validation of AI-assisted outputs.

The baseline is Evidence in SECAV-O. It is not a universal performance score and should not be used as an isolated ranking mechanism.

## 4. Ongoing coaching

The baseline feeds the Annual Coaching Plan and Coaching Sprints. Sprint retrospectives review progress, risks, evidence and alignment with institutional/project objectives. Replanning adjusts objectives and interventions based on evidence.

## 5. New-member onboarding

When a new member joins an active team, the coach supports structured onboarding. At minimum, onboarding should provide:

- project purpose and current state;
- institutional and project objectives;
- architecture / technical context relevant to the role;
- current major risks, preventive actions and contingency/corrective responses;
- current engineering and quality practices;
- review, inspection and unit-testing expectations;
- AI-assistance / human-validation expectations;
- active coaching priorities and team learning patterns; and
- access to relevant artifacts and references.

A member-specific baseline is then established and the coaching plan is updated. The new member should not inherit another member's competency assumptions or baseline.

## 6. Traceability

The lifecycle should be traceable as:

`Institutional/Project Objective -> Team Launch -> Member Preparation -> Baseline -> Coaching Objective -> Competency -> Engineering Activity -> Artifact -> Evidence/Metric -> Coaching Intervention -> Retrospective -> Replanning`

For later joins:

`New-Member Onboarding -> Project/Risk Context -> Member Baseline -> Updated Coaching Objectives -> Sprint Evidence -> Competency Trend`

## Ethics and boundary

Member preparation and onboarding must follow the SECAV-O Coach Code of Ethics. Coaching evidence should be collected transparently, proportionately and for legitimate developmental/engineering purposes. The coach should distinguish developmental coaching from formal employment-performance decisions unless the governance model explicitly assigns and discloses that role.
