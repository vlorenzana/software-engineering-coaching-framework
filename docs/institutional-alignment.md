# SECAV-O Institutional Alignment Model

## Purpose

Coaching should improve engineer capability while contributing to legitimate organizational goals. SECAV-O therefore requires explicit traceability between coaching objectives and relevant institutional objectives where appropriate.

## Alignment chain

Institutional Objective -> Team / Engineering Objective -> Coaching Objective -> Competency -> Engineering Activity -> Evidence / Metric -> Sprint Result

## Examples of institutional objectives

- improve software quality;
- reduce late-phase defects;
- strengthen secure-development practices;
- increase reliability of peer review and code inspection;
- improve unit-test quality and earlier defect detection;
- strengthen architecture and design capability;
- preserve accountable human validation in AI-assisted development;
- build internal technical leadership and reviewer capability;
- support workforce development and professional growth.

## Alignment rules

1. A coaching objective should identify the institutional or team objective it supports, when one exists.
2. The coaching objective must remain a developmental objective, not merely a delivery target.
3. Metrics should distinguish organizational outcomes from individual competency evidence.
4. Institutional alignment must not override professional ethics, confidentiality, fairness, or engineer development.
5. If an institutional objective conflicts with ethical coaching practice, the coach documents the conflict and escalates appropriately.

## Example

**Institutional objective:** Reduce late-phase design defects.

**Coaching objective:** Improve the engineer's independent Design Review competency.

**Evidence:** peer-review records, inspection findings, defect phase-injected / phase-detected data, acceptance-criteria results.

**Metric:** trend in design defects escaping into integration/system testing.

**Development result:** movement from “Performs with Review” toward “Independent” or “Can Review Others,” supported by repeated evidence.
