# SECAV-O Coaching Risk Management

## Purpose

Risk management is part of the coaching plan rather than an administrative add-on. The Annual Coaching Plan maintains a risk register, and every Coaching Sprint retrospective reviews whether risks changed, materialized, were mitigated, or require new actions.

## Risk model

Each risk should include:

- Risk ID
- Description
- Category
- Cause / trigger
- Probability of occurrence
- Impact if it occurs
- Overall priority / exposure
- Preventive action(s)
- Corrective / contingency action(s)
- Risk owner
- Related coaching objective or institutional objective
- Status
- Last review date
- Evidence / notes

## Suggested qualitative scales

### Probability

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| Low | Unlikely under current conditions. |
| Medium | Plausible and should be monitored. |
| High | Likely or already showing indicators. |

### Impact

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| Low | Limited effect on a sprint objective or local activity. |
| Medium | Material effect on coaching progress, quality, evidence collection, or team objectives. |
| High | Could invalidate a key objective, create significant quality/security exposure, or materially impair the coaching plan. |

The framework may optionally use numeric scoring, but it does not prescribe a universal formula.

## Preventive and corrective actions

**Preventive actions** reduce the probability that a risk occurs. Examples include clarifying acceptance criteria, scheduling peer reviews, providing training before a new activity, increasing review frequency, or obtaining needed access/data early.

**Corrective / contingency actions** define what to do if the risk materializes. Examples include changing the sprint objective, adding an independent reviewer, introducing an additional test activity, escalating a dependency, or revising the baseline.

## Retrospective integration

Every Sprint Retrospective should answer:

1. Which risks were reviewed?
2. Did probability or impact change?
3. Which preventive actions were completed?
4. Did any risk materialize?
5. Were corrective actions activated?
6. Were new risks discovered?
7. Which risks carry forward to the next sprint?

## Coaching-specific examples

- insufficient evidence to assess a competency;
- metrics that create misleading incentives;
- late-phase defect recurrence;
- lack of peer-review participation;
- insufficient unit-test evidence;
- over-reliance on AI-generated outputs without adequate human validation;
- lack of time or access to relevant engineering artifacts;
- conflict between coaching objectives and institutional priorities;
- confidentiality or privacy constraints affecting evidence collection.
