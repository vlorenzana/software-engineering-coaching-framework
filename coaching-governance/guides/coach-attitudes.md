# Coach Attitudes: Posture and Ideal State Guide

## Purpose

This guide describes the attitudes the coach must sustain throughout the human development processes they lead. Each attitude is an observable posture standard — verifiable by the coach through self-reflection, peer supervision, or review by the Coaches Committee.

Unlike `coaching-governance/guides/coach-team-behaviors.md`, which describes what to observe in the team leader, this guide describes the internal state and conduct the coach must cultivate and maintain.

> The coach cannot guide from outside the attitudes described here. The coach's posture is part of the method, not a prerequisite to it.

---

## 1. Active Listening

The coach pays full attention not only to the words, but to the emotions and silences of the coachee — without planning a response while the other person speaks.

**Indicators of effective practice:**

- The coach can repeat or paraphrase what the coachee just expressed before responding.
- The coach detects and names the underlying emotion when it diverges from the verbal content. (*"I notice you say it's fine, but there's something more there."*)
- The coach tolerates silences without filling them prematurely.
- The coach's next question emerges from what the coachee said, not from a prepared script.
- The coach does not interrupt or complete the coachee's sentences.

**Signs the attitude is being lost:**

- The coach asks questions that were prepared before the coachee finished speaking.
- The questions bear no direct relation to what the coachee just expressed.
- The coach ends sessions without having noticed changes in tone, pace, or emotion in the coachee.
- The coach verbalizes solutions or interpretations before verifying whether the coachee shares them.

---

## 2. Suspension of Judgment

The coach maintains an open, neutral mind. The goal is to understand the coachee's perspective, not to evaluate whether it is right or wrong.

**Indicators of effective practice:**

- The coach explores the coachee's perspective before offering any reframe or alternative.
- When a perspective arises that the coach does not share, they note it internally without expressing it as a correction.
- The coach asks questions that expand the coachee's map, not questions that direct them toward the coach's conclusion.
- The coach distinguishes between "observing a pattern" and "judging a person."
- Competency assessment is based on observable evidence (`secav:Evidence`), never on impressions or assumptions.

**Signs the attitude is being lost:**

- The coach responds with phrases implying a value judgment: *"That's not right,"* *"You should...,"* *"The correct thing is..."*
- The coach closes off exploration prematurely because they already have a hypothesis.
- The coach's interventions follow a pattern of validation or invalidation instead of exploration.
- The coach expresses surprise or disapproval at the coachee's decisions.

---

## 3. Genuine Curiosity

The coach asks open questions from a place of sincere interest in discovering another person's potential — avoiding directing the solution.

**Indicators of effective practice:**

- The coach's questions are open and do not contain the expected answer embedded in their phrasing.
- The coach asks questions they could not answer themselves — because they arise from the coachee's territory, not the coach's.
- The coach revises their working hypothesis when the coachee provides information that contradicts it.
- Sessions end with the coachee having discovered something they had not articulated at the start.
- The coach tolerates not knowing where the conversation is going as it unfolds.

**Signs the attitude is being lost:**

- The questions have an obvious implied answer: *"Don't you think it would be better if...?"*
- The coach anticipates conclusions before the coachee has reached them.
- The coach steers the session toward a destination they chose before it began.
- The questions are closed and verificatory, not exploratory.

---

## 4. Authentic Empathy

The coach connects with the person's experience to validate their feelings — creating an environment of absolute safety and trust.

**Indicators of effective practice:**

- The coach validates the coachee's emotion before moving to analysis or action. (*"It makes sense that this generated frustration for you."*)
- The coachee expresses difficult topics without signs of self-censorship or anticipation of the coach's judgment.
- The coach distinguishes between empathy — *"I understand what you feel"* — and sympathy — *"That would happen to me too"* — and operates from the former.
- The coach can name the emotion they observe without projecting their own experience onto the coachee.
- The coachee reports feeling understood, not advised, during sessions.

**Signs the attitude is being lost:**

- The coach moves to problem-solving without having acknowledged the coachee's emotional state.
- The coach shares their own experience in response to the coachee's before exploring the coachee's.
- The coachee minimizes or rationalizes their emotions in the coach's presence.
- The coach reframes or relativizes the coachee's feelings instead of validating them.

---

## 5. Full Presence

The coach is 100% available in the here and now during the session — eliminating physical and mental distractors.

**Indicators of effective practice:**

- The coach has no active devices or visible notifications during the session.
- The coach enters each session having made a conscious transition from their previous activity — they do not carry the mental state of another meeting.
- The coach can detect subtle changes in the coachee's body language, speech pace, or tone.
- If the coach notices their attention drifted, they acknowledge it and return it without self-criticism.
- At the end of the session, the coach can describe with precision the key moments that occurred.

**Signs the attitude is being lost:**

- The coach checks the clock or phone during the session.
- The coach loses the thread and asks the coachee to repeat something without a justified distraction having occurred.
- The coach mentally plans the next session or task while the coachee speaks.
- The coach's post-session notes are vague or general compared to the richness of what occurred.

---

## 6. Achievement Orientation

The coach keeps focus on the future and on concrete actions — driving the individual's autonomy and accountability, not dependence on the process.

**Indicators of effective practice:**

- At the end of each session there are specific, verifiable commitments formulated by the coachee, not the coach.
- The coach links agreed actions to the recorded development objectives (`secav:CoachingIntervention`, `secavo:ImprovementAction`).
- The coach distinguishes between exploring the past to understand a pattern and remaining anchored in it.
- The coachee can articulate their own next steps without depending on the coach to formulate them.
- The coach celebrates the coachee's progress by attributing it to the coachee, not to the sessions.

**Signs the attitude is being lost:**

- Sessions end without concrete commitments or with commitments formulated by the coach.
- Session time is concentrated on analyzing the past without deriving future action.
- The coachee reports feeling motivated during the session but does not know what to do when leaving.
- The coach assumes responsibility for the coachee's progress instead of returning it to them.

## 7. Per-session self-verification checklist

The coach completes this list at the end of each session as a reflection instrument. Items marked "No" or "Partial" are candidates for exploration in peer supervision or the Coaches Committee.

| Attitude | Yes | Partial | No | Not evaluated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I listened without planning my response while the coachee spoke | | | | |
| I was able to paraphrase what the coachee expressed before responding | | | | |
| I did not emit a value judgment — I explored before reframing | | | | |
| My questions arose from the coachee's territory, not my script | | | | |
| I validated the coachee's emotional state before moving to analysis | | | | |
| I was free of physical and mental distractors throughout the session | | | | |
| The closing commitments were formulated by the coachee, not me | | | | |
| The coachee left with clarity about their next steps | | | | |
